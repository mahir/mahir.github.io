One shop, seven feeds

Similar items

Compares each listing with your favorites: craft, shape, surface, color, price, shop. It needs no other shoppers, and it narrows fast.

Shoppers like you

Looks at the shoppers who favorited what you did, and at what else they favorited. It finds the rug that goes with the mug, and it cannot see a listing nobody has favorited.

Learned taste

Gives every shopper and listing a place in a space learned from all the favorites, and looks near yours.

Explore

Starts from your taste, keeps repeats apart, and gives three places to listings it knows little about.

The blend

Takes candidates from the other feeds, scores them once, and sells two places.

How each feed decides

Most popular
Sorts by the number of favorites. It needs to know nothing about you, which is why it is what a new visitor gets. What is already seen gets seen more.
Trending and new
A favorite counts half as much for every week that has passed. A listing in its first week has nothing to count, so it is lifted: most on its first day, and more if its shop’s other listings are liked.
Similar items
Content-based filtering. Your favorites are averaged into a profile, attribute by attribute, and each listing is scored by the cosine between it and that profile. It can recommend a listing nobody has seen. It cannot surprise you.
Shoppers like you
Item-to-item collaborative filtering. Two listings are near when the same shoppers favorited both; each of your favorites votes for its neighbors. It never reads what a listing is, so it crosses from mugs to rugs when shoppers do.
Learned taste
Matrix factorization. From every shopper’s favorites, alternating least squares gives each listing eight numbers, so that listings liked by the same shoppers point the same way. Your own eight are fitted in the browser from your favorites, and a listing’s score is the product of the two.
Explore
Seven places go to your taste, each pick marked down for resembling your favorites and the picks above it. Three go to tries: listings with few favorites, from crafts you have not favorited, with a bonus that shrinks as favorites come in. A feed that never tries anything never learns anything new.
The blend
How a shop’s front page is put together. Candidates come from the other feeds; one score weighs taste, popularity and freshness; look-alikes are spaced out; then two places go to the shops that bid the most for a click, weighted by how well the listing fits you.

Nothing here is real. The listings, the shops and the shoppers are generated from a seed, and the favorites are simulated: each shopper has one or two of eight hidden tastes, and a taste spans crafts. The seven feeds are not simulated. They run in this page, on that data, as you click.
