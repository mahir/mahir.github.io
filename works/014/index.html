Fifteen minutes on foot

Twelve cities at the same scale. In each one a walker leaves a well-known spot at 5 km/h, and the streets light up as they are reached. The ring is fifteen minutes in a straight line. The number is how much of the ring the streets let you reach.

Istanbul

Eminönü

61%3.0 km²

c. 657 BCE · grown · port

Byzantion was founded by Greek colonists, by tradition around 657 BCE. Eminönü is its harbour side: the Golden Horn lies to the north, and from here the walk crosses it by the Galata Bridge alone.

Paris

Notre-Dame

78%3.8 km²

3rd century BCE · grown · inland

Settled by the Parisii from the 3rd century BCE. The start is the point zéro in front of Notre-Dame, from which France measures its roads. Haussmann cut his boulevards through the city between 1853 and 1870.

London

Trafalgar Square

73%3.6 km²

c. 47 CE · grown · port

Roman Londinium dates from about 47 CE. London has grown street by street since: Wren’s plan to rebuild it after the fire of 1666 was never adopted.

Venice

Rialto Bridge

50%2.4 km²

421, by tradition · grown · port

Founded, by tradition, in 421; the Rialto has been its centre since about 810. There are no cars, and every canal has to be crossed by a bridge.

Fez

al-Qarawiyyin

57%2.8 km²

789 · grown · inland

The medina, Fes el-Bali, was founded by the Idrisids between 789 and 808. Most of its lanes are too narrow for a car.

Kyoto

Shijō Karasuma

71%3.5 km²

794 · planned · inland

Laid out in 794 as Heian-kyō, on a grid modelled on the Chinese capital Chang’an.

Beijing

Drum Tower

59%2.9 km²

1267 · planned · inland

Dadu, Kublai Khan’s capital, was laid out from 1267 and rebuilt as the Ming capital by 1420. The Drum Tower stands at the north end of the city’s central axis.

New York

Times Square

75%3.7 km²

1811 · planned · port

Dutch from 1624. The grid from Houston Street to 155th Street was drawn in the Commissioners’ Plan of 1811. Broadway is older than the grid and cuts across it here.

Barcelona

Plaça de Catalunya

79%3.9 km²

1859 · planned · port

Roman Barcino dates from the late 1st century BCE. The square is where the old town meets the Eixample, Ildefons Cerdà’s grid, approved in 1859.

Tokyo

Shibuya Crossing

77%3.8 km²

1885 · grown · port

Edo Castle dates from 1457. Shibuya was still a group of villages outside the city when its station opened in 1885.

Brasília

The cathedral

56%2.8 km²

1957 · planned · inland

Lúcio Costa’s Plano Piloto won the competition in 1957 and the city was inaugurated in 1960. The start is the cathedral, on the Monumental Axis.

Dubai

Burj Khalifa / Dubai Mall station

40%2.0 km²

2004 · planned · port

Downtown Dubai has been built since 2004 around Burj Khalifa, which opened in 2010. The start is its metro station, where a covered footbridge leaves for the mall. The corridors inside the mall are not counted.

What shows up

From these twelve starts, age and planning decide little. The six cities that reach the most are three that grew, Paris, Tokyo and London, and three that were drawn, Barcelona, New York and Kyoto. A grid leaves a diamond, a tangle leaves a blot, and the two cover about the same ground.

What takes ground away is whatever can’t be walked through. Water does it in Venice and Istanbul, and long blocks do it in Beijing and Dubai.

How it is counted

The streets and paths are OpenStreetMap’s. The walker moves at 5 km/h along anything open to people on foot and nowhere else: no ferries, no lifts and no shortcuts through buildings. Hills, stairs and traffic lights don’t slow the walk down.

Ground within 50 m of a street that has been reached counts as reached. The ring has a radius of 1.25 km and holds 4.9 km², so 50% means 2.45 km² of city in fifteen minutes. Water, walls, railways and long blocks all take ground away. A grid takes some too: on a square grid a walker can’t cut the corner.

Open a city to drag the map and start somewhere else, up to 1.2 km from the first spot. The year under each city is when its streets began: the founding for cities that grew, the plan for cities that were drawn.
