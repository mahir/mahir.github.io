No. 016 · Mahir Yavuz

A coffee, ordered by colour

A mokka stands on a marble table, on a silver tray, with a jug of milk and a glass of water beside it. Choose a single or a double, pour milk until the cup looks like the coffee you drink every day, stir, and say it is yours. A card of colours then comes to the table, from black to milky white, and shows which colour your coffee is and what to ask for in Vienna. The card has five names on it, and each one you find is written beside its colours. You can also point at a shade on the card, and the coffee is mixed to it.

The card on this page is its own. Two Viennese sources describe ordering by a colour card; neither shows one, and no surviving card was found.

Milk scatters light and coffee absorbs it, so the first drops of milk change the colour most. The cup and the card are coloured by the same formula (Kubelka–Munk), not by blending two colours.

A coffee, ordered by colour

No. 016 by Mahir Yavuz. A mokka stands on a marble table, in a white porcelain cup on a small oval silver tray, with a teaspoon, a porcelain milk jug and a glass of water. The visitor chooses a single or a double mokka, holds the jug to pour milk, a few drops or a stream, and drags in the cup to stir. When the cup looks like the coffee they drink every day, they say it is theirs.

A card of nine painted colours, from black to milky white, then comes to the table, and a ring settles on the colour of their coffee. The page names it, and tells them what to ask for in Vienna: a Kleiner or Großer Schwarzer, a Kapuziner, a Kleiner or Großer Brauner, a Melange, or a Kaffee verkehrt. Each name they find is written on the card beside its colours. The visitor can also point at a shade on the card, and the coffee is mixed to it.

The card on this page is its own. Two Viennese sources describe ordering by a colour card; neither shows one, and no surviving card was found.

Milk scatters light and coffee absorbs it, so the first drops of milk change the colour most. The cup and the card are coloured by the same formula (Kubelka–Munk), not by blending two colours.

Sources: Fabia, on the colour card; Vienna Pass, on the colour card and the names; Vollpension, the coffee lexicon.
