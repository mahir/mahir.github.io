Turkish, suffix by suffix

No. 013 by Mahir Yavuz. Turkish builds a long word by adding suffixes to a root, one after another, and each suffix adds one piece of meaning. This page builds one word that way. It opens on the root, Çekoslovakya, with its English under it, Czechoslovakia. Each tap adds the next suffix, and the English is rewritten to match. After ten suffixes the word has 44 letters and its English is a whole sentence.

  1. Çekoslovakya: Czechoslovakia
  2. Çekoslovakyalı: Czechoslovakian
  3. Çekoslovakyalılaş: to become Czechoslovakian
  4. Çekoslovakyalılaştır: to make someone Czechoslovakian
  5. Çekoslovakyalılaştırama: to be unable to make someone Czechoslovakian
  6. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadık: one that could not be made Czechoslovakian
  7. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadıklar: those that could not be made Czechoslovakian
  8. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadıklarımız: those that we could not make Czechoslovakian
  9. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadıklarımızdan: one of those that we could not make Czechoslovakian
  10. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadıklarımızdanmış: is reportedly one of those that we could not make Czechoslovakian
  11. Çekoslovakyalılaştıramadıklarımızdanmışsınız: you are reportedly one of those that we could not make Czechoslovakian

Each suffix has its own colour, and the English words it adds are in the same colour. Turkish adds at the end of the word; English has to be rewritten from the front.

The vowels in the suffixes are not fixed. Each one follows the vowel before it, which is called vowel harmony, so the root’s last vowel sets all of them. The root can be changed to Afyonkarahisar, İzmir or Bodrum, or to any place the visitor types, and every suffix changes its vowels to match: after İzmir, lı becomes li, laş becomes leş and dık becomes dik.
