A print shop that does not close. Each sheet is a poster of a city, drawn on the page as you watch: the sky and the water first, then the city from the far end to the near, then the boats, the lettering and the frame. It stays a while, and the next sheet comes.

Every city is printed the way posters of it have been made: New York as an engraved bird's-eye view, Tokyo as a woodblock, Paris in gouache, and so on, each with its own inks and its own type.

It began with a vintage-style poster of New York. Everything is drawn in the browser. Nothing is stored.

    Cities, in print

    No. 024 by Mahir Yavuz. A sequence of city posters, each drawn on the page in about fifteen seconds and then left for a while before the next sheet slides over it.

    Each city is drawn in a print tradition of its own, with its own inks and its own lettering. The posters, in the order they are printed:

    1. New York. An engraved bird's-eye view of Manhattan from above the harbor, with the Empire State Building between the words of the title, the Chrysler Building, the Brooklyn Bridge, the Statue of Liberty and a ferry.
    2. Tokyo. A woodblock print at dusk: Mount Fuji and a red sun over tiled roofs, Tokyo Tower, a five-story pagoda, an arched bridge over the river, a cherry branch and a red seal.
    3. Paris. A gouache view along the Seine with the Eiffel Tower, Sacré-Cœur against a low sun, Notre-Dame, the Invalides dome and rows of zinc roofs, the name in a looping script.
    4. Mexico City. A folk painting of Mexico City under strings of papel picado: the Palacio de Bellas Artes with its orange tiled dome among purple jacarandas, the Angel of Independence on her column, the Torre Latinoamericana and the Metropolitan Cathedral, the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl, a crowd of round mural figures carrying calla lilies, and trajineras with painted arches on a canal, in a border of serape stripes.
    5. Cairo. The Nile with feluccas and palms, the Cairo Tower and the Citadel's mosque against a rayed sky and a great sun, the pyramids beyond, the name in English and Arabic.
    6. London. A railway-poster view of the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben across the Thames, with Westminster Bridge, a red bus, a lamp post and St Paul's dome far off, under a red title band.
    7. Sydney. The harbor at Sydney at sundown in 1930s poster style, the Harbour Bridge's arch with the sun set inside it and rays fanning out behind, the Opera House's sails, the city's towers and Sydney Tower, Fort Denison, ferries and yachts on the water, and a red gum with cockatoos.
    8. İstanbul. A night view of the old city across the Bosphorus, its mosques and domes under a crescent moon, the Galata Tower, the Maiden's Tower and a ferry, in a border of tulips and carnations like İznik tile.
    9. Rio de Janeiro. A view over Copacabana's wave-patterned promenade and beach to Sugarloaf Mountain, with Christ the Redeemer on Corcovado against the sun, palms and sailboats.
    10. Venice. Santa Maria della Salute, the Campanile and the Doge's Palace across the lagoon at a pink sunset, with gondolas, a sailing barge and striped mooring poles, framed by an ogee arch.
    11. Seoul. A folk painting of Seoul in the minhwa manner: Gyeongbokgung's Geunjeongjeon hall and Gwanghwamun gate among a town of tiled roofs, Bugaksan and Inwangsan behind, hills striped like a tiger, Namsan with N Seoul Tower, curling clouds, a river of scalloped waves with two mandarin ducks, peonies by a garden rock and a magpie in a pine.

    14. Marrakech.     16. Washington. A 1930s-style silkscreen of Washington from the air above the Capitol dome, looking west along the National Mall past the Smithsonian Castle and the museums to the Washington Monument, the Reflecting Pool and the Lincoln Memorial, with the Jefferson Memorial on the Tidal Basin among cherry trees, and across the Potomac the Memorial Bridge, Arlington National Cemetery and Arlington House on its hill, Rosslyn's towers and Georgetown, over the name WASHINGTON.
