Imaginary vehicles, drawn to scale

No. 018 by Mahir Yavuz. An endless drafting table. On every sheet the page invents a vehicle and gives it a name. It letters the vehicle's class and name in the title block first, then draws it in ink as a technical illustration seen from above at an angle: construction lines, then the structure part by part, then the detail and the hatching, then the dimensions. There are 31 kinds of vehicle. Some are plain: landers, rovers, crawlers, locomotives, airships, tugs, rockets. Most combine things that do not belong together: a high-heeled shoe on three wheels, a teapot under a wing, a grand piano on walking legs, a typewriter on tracks, a lighthouse on pontoons, a carousel under rotors, a car carrying forty loudspeakers, a bus with five decks, a tugboat on six legs, a steam locomotive built as a flying boat. No two sheets are the same.

Numbered balloons point at the main parts, and a parts list names them. The title block gives the drawing number, the scale with a scale bar, the date and the vehicle's length, height, mass and crew. When the sheet is finished it stays for a few seconds, then slides away, and the next sheet begins.

Each sheet has a number. The address ends with it, so a sheet can be shared and opened again. Space pauses the pen, the right arrow turns to the next sheet, and Save PNG keeps the finished sheet as a picture.

Everything is drawn in the browser, without libraries. The lettering is a single-stroke technical hand made for this page.
